Rules:

1. Every claim must cite one or more evidence_ids.
2. Never use information outside SUPPORTED EVIDENCE.
3. Never invent or estimate numbers.
4. Preserve method names exactly.
5. Preserve comparison direction exactly.
6. Do not reinterpret metrics.
7. Keep each claim concise enough for a poster.
8. Prefer one scientific assertion per claim.
9. Avoid generic filler such as:
   "significant improvements"
   unless the supplied evidence explicitly supports it.
10. If evidence is insufficient for a claim, omit the claim.
11. In inline formulas, write multiplication explicitly with the × sign;
    never use *, ·, or implicit multiplication between alpha and max_{...}.
{% if family_mode %}
12. Write as a research poster, not a review of the paper. Prefer the method,
    dataset, experiment or finding as subject. Avoid "The authors propose/report"
    and do not assume an author identity by inserting "we".
13. Concise means information-rich and understandable, not cryptic. Preserve
    necessary definitions, experimental conditions, metrics, comparison targets,
    uncertainty and limitations. Explain the essential mechanism when supported.
14. Do not add generic impact language, repeat a finding, or add tangential
    details just to fill space. Order prerequisites/settings before dependent
    findings, unless a clearly understandable takeaway-first structure is used.
15. State the actual scientific content directly: what the method does, how its
    essential mechanism works, or what was observed under which conditions and
    against which comparator. Include those elements only when supported and
    necessary, not as a mandatory sentence template. Avoid empty transitions
    such as "It is important to note" and unjustified evaluative adjectives.
16. Preserve attribution when describing related work or another study. Do not
    mechanically delete author names, insert "we", turn an association into a
    cause, or make a qualified result universal. Prefer understandable connected
    explanation over cryptic fragments or disconnected abstract-summary sentences.
    Do not generate "Topic:" cues or formatting inside scientific claim text;
    Curator separately chooses optional display organization.
